CONCEPT-SAFE-001

2. Safety and maintenance levels

Only personnel qualified for the assigned maintenance level may work on the pump. A normal stop does not isolate electrical energy, stored vacuum, or process gas. Apply the site lockout/tagout procedure and verify a zero-energy state before opening the guard, casing, seal chamber, or terminal box.

WARNING · Rotating and stored-energy hazards. Stop the drive. Isolate electrical and process sources. Vent the casing through the designated system connection. Verify zero voltage, zero gauge differential, and zero shaft rotation before disassembly.
WARNING · Lifting hazard. The assembled unit has a maximum mass of 328 kg. Use the baseplate lifting lugs and a rated lift plan. Do not lift by the motor eye, guard, or pipe nozzles.

Hot surfaces: Let the casing and bearing carrier cool below 45 °C before contact. Process material: Identify residues and use the site's handling controls before opening the casing.

2.1 Make the pump safe for maintenance TASK-SAFE-001

Prerequisites: Work order, model and serial number, isolation diagram, process-material assessment, and competent personnel. Tools: Rated voltage tester, calibrated pressure gauge, lockout kit. Consumables: Identification tags. PPE: Safety glasses, protective gloves, safety footwear; add process-specific protection from the site assessment.

  1. Record the model, serial number, configuration, and work order.
  2. Stop the pump by the site operating sequence. Wait until the shaft stops.
  3. Isolate and lock out every electrical source. Have a qualified electrician verify absence of voltage.
  4. Close the process isolation valves. Vent through the designated connection and verify zero gauge differential.
  5. Check the surface temperature. Continue only when exposed parts are below 45 °C.
  6. Attach the isolation record to the work order. Keep the keys under site control.

Acceptance: Isolation record complete; zero voltage and zero gauge differential recorded; shaft stationary. If any condition is not met, stop the task.

3. System concepts

3.1 Operating principle

The motor turns a single side-channel impeller through a flexible coupling. The rotating blades transfer energy to dry air inside the annular casing. The inlet pressure falls below ambient pressure and air leaves through the discharge. The impeller does not contact the casing in normal operation. The bearing carrier supports the shaft; the shaft seal limits leakage at the casing boundary.

3.2 Air-flow theory

At the rated free-flow point, the pump moves 450 m³/h with no inlet restriction. As inlet vacuum increases, delivered flow falls. Continuous operation is limited to −28 kPa gauge inlet pressure. The unit is for dry, nonflammable air; solids or liquid carryover can damage the impeller and seal.

3.3 System architecture and lubrication

The motor, guard, pump, and baseplate form one skid. The oil-bath bearing carrier is separate from the dry process-air path. A sight glass provides the static oil-level reference. Model C adds vibration points P1 and P2; it does not change the mechanical service limits.

5.1 Routine work packages

Daily · TASK-MAINT-001 · Operator

Tools: log sheet, infrared thermometer, vibration meter. Consumables: none. PPE: safety glasses, hearing protection, safety footwear.

  1. Read the vacuum, current, and hour meter at steady load.
  2. Measure temperatures and vibration at the points in Figure 7-1.
  3. Check the guard, seal area, and oil sight glass without removing covers.
  4. Record values; use Table 4-2 to classify the condition.

Acceptance: No leaks or damaged guard; values below alarm limits.

Weekly · TASK-MAINT-002 · Operator

Tools: flashlight, inspection mirror. Consumables: lint-free wipes. PPE: safety glasses, gloves, hearing protection.

  1. Inspect accessible piping joints and the intake screen.
  2. Record corrosion, loose supports, deposits, or damage.
  3. Compare findings with the previous inspection record.

Acceptance: No new leakage, blockage, or loose support.

Monthly · TASK-MAINT-003 · Field

Tools: calibrated vibration meter, thermometer. Consumables: record labels. PPE: safety glasses, hearing protection, safety footwear.

  1. Measure P1/P2 in three axes at steady load.
  2. Measure bearing, motor-frame, and casing temperatures.
  3. Compare readings with Table 4-2 and the last three monthly results.

Acceptance: All values below alarm thresholds; investigate a rising trend.

Quarterly · TASK-MAINT-004 · Field

Tools: dial indicator or laser alignment tool, torque wrench, flashlight. Consumables: replacement coupling insert if needed. PPE: safety glasses, gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Complete TASK-SAFE-001.
  2. Inspect the seal, coupling, and motor by Sections 7.2–7.4.
  3. Measure alignment by Section 7.6; restore the guard.

Acceptance: Wear and alignment within Table 4-4.

Six months · TASK-MAINT-005 · Field

Tools: drain pan, measuring jug, torque wrench. Consumables: 0.75 L ISO VG 68 PAO oil, item 49 drain-plug seal, wipes. PPE: safety glasses, oil-resistant gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Complete TASK-SAFE-001 and place a drain pan under the carrier.
  2. Drain oil and inspect it for water or metal.
  3. Fit a new item 49 seal. Torque item 27 plug to 30 N·m.
  4. Refill with the oil in Table 4-5 to sight-glass centre.
  5. Record oil type, quantity, date, and hour-meter reading.

Acceptance: No leakage; static level at centre ±5 mm.

Annual · TASK-MAINT-006 · Depot

Tools: bearing puller, dial indicator, depth gauge, feeler gauge, torque wrenches. Consumables: bearing set, seal kit by effectivity, gaskets, ISO VG 68 oil. PPE: safety glasses, gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Complete TASK-SAFE-001; remove the pump by Section 8.1.
  2. Inspect bearings, seal, shaft, and impeller by Section 7.
  3. Replace parts at their service limits; rebuild by Section 8.
  4. Complete alignment, functional test, and return-to-service record.

Acceptance: Installation limits in Table 4-4 and operating limits in Table 4-2.

TASK-OPS-001 · TASK-COMM-001

6. Operation and commissioning

6.1 Start and stop the pump

Level: Operator. Prerequisites: Work area clear, guards fitted, process connections secure, oil at the level in Table 4-5. Tools: Operating log, vibration meter. Consumables: none. PPE: Safety glasses, hearing protection, safety footwear.

  1. Record the model, serial number, hour meter, and process configuration.
  2. Check that the inlet and discharge valves are in the site's approved start position.
  3. Confirm that the oil sight glass shows the required static level.
  4. Start the motor from the control station. Confirm the rotation indicator during the permitted observation period.
  5. At steady load, record inlet vacuum, current, vibration, and temperatures. Compare them with Tables 4-1 and 4-2.
  6. To stop, follow the site's process shutdown sequence. Confirm zero shaft rotation before leaving the unit.

Acceptance: Guard fitted, no visible leak, and all measured values below the alarm limits. If a stop limit is exceeded, stop the pump and follow Section 9.

6.2 Commission after service

Level: Field. References: Sections 2.1, 4, 7.6, and 12.1. Tools: Calibrated vibration meter, thermometer, current meter, alignment instrument. Consumables: maintenance record sheet. PPE: Safety glasses, hearing protection, safety footwear.

  1. Check that the work package and part traceability are complete.
  2. Verify the oil level, coupling alignment, fastener torque, guard installation, and electrical-test record.
  3. Remove tools and temporary labels from the work area.
  4. Release isolation under site control and start the pump by Section 6.1.
  5. Run at steady normal load for 20 minutes. Record P1/P2 vibration, three temperatures, inlet vacuum, and motor current.
  6. Inspect the seal, oil carrier, and process joints for leakage.
  7. Record the results and obtain the maintenance release signature on Form 12-1.

Acceptance: All readings within Table 4-2 normal range; no leakage; no abnormal sound; release record complete.

TASK-INSP-001 THROUGH TASK-INSP-006

7. Inspection tasks

Record every measurement with its location, instrument ID, operating state, and limit. Figure 7-1 identifies the points used in this section. Use the service limit only for an isolated unit; use Table 4-2 for running condition.

7.1 Bearing inspection TASK-INSP-001

Level: Field; depot for bearing removal. Prerequisites: TASK-SAFE-001 complete; oil drained for internal inspection. Tools: Dial indicator, magnetic base, feeler gauge, flashlight. Consumables: Lint-free wipes, ISO VG 68 oil, replacement carrier gasket. PPE: Safety glasses, gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Record P1 and P2 vibration and bearing temperature before stopping the unit.
  2. Isolate the unit. Check the oil for water, metal, or discoloration.
  3. Mount the indicator at the shaft end. Measure radial movement at both bearing positions.
  4. Rotate the shaft by hand. Check for roughness, binding, or abnormal sound.
  5. Inspect the carrier for cracks, oil leakage, and damaged seats.
  6. Record readings and compare them with Table 4-4.

Acceptance: Radial play below 0.10 mm, smooth rotation, no carrier damage or contamination. Replace the bearing set if a limit is reached.

7.2 Mechanical seal inspection TASK-INSP-002

Level: Field. Prerequisites: TASK-SAFE-001 complete; identify seal cartridge by Table 1-2. Tools: Depth gauge, flashlight, inspection mirror. Consumables: Lint-free wipes, compatible cleaning solvent, O-rings by effectivity. PPE: Safety glasses, chemical-resistant gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Record the observed leak location before removing the seal.
  2. Remove the cartridge by Section 8.2. Keep the faces clean.
  3. Inspect the stationary and rotating faces for chips, scoring, or heat marks.
  4. Measure the deepest wear track with a depth gauge.
  5. Inspect the sleeve and O-ring grooves for damage.
  6. Record the part number, wear depth, and disposition.

Acceptance: Wear below 0.25 mm; no cracks, chips, or damaged elastomers. Replace a damaged cartridge as a set.

7.3 Motor inspection TASK-INSP-003

Level: Field electrical. Prerequisites: TASK-SAFE-001 complete; electrical test plan available. Tools: Approved voltage tester, insulation tester, low-resistance meter, thermometer, torque wrench. Consumables: Terminal labels, lint-free wipes. PPE: Arc-rated protection as required by the site, safety glasses, insulated gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Record running current and frame temperature before stopping.
  2. Verify zero voltage at the motor terminals.
  3. Inspect terminals, grounding, cooling fan, and ventilation openings.
  4. Have qualified electrical personnel complete the three checks in Table 4-6.
  5. Inspect motor feet and record the mounting-bolt condition.

Acceptance: No loose connection or blocked ventilation; frame temperature below 80 °C during normal load; electrical results within Table 4-6 limits.

7.4 Coupling inspection TASK-INSP-004

Level: Field. Prerequisites: TASK-SAFE-001 complete; guard removed after rotation stops. Tools: Feeler gauge, straightedge, dial indicator, torque wrench. Consumables: Replacement elastomer insert. PPE: Safety glasses, gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Mark the installed hub positions and record the gap.
  2. Inspect the insert for cracks, hardening, or missing material.
  3. Inspect hub teeth, keys, set screws, and guard for damage.
  4. Measure parallel offset and angular difference by Section 7.6.
  5. Restore the guard with item 45 screws at 10 N·m.

Acceptance: Insert intact; hubs secure; cold alignment within Table 4-4; guard fitted.

7.5 Impeller inspection TASK-INSP-005

Level: Depot. Prerequisites: TASK-SAFE-001 complete; casing drained and opened by Section 8.6. Tools: Feeler gauges, dial indicator, borescope, flashlight. Consumables: New cover gasket, lint-free wipes. PPE: Safety glasses, cut-resistant gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Mark the rotation direction and impeller position before removal.
  2. Inspect blades and side channel for impact marks, corrosion, or rubbing.
  3. Measure the axial gap at four quadrants.
  4. Measure shaft runout at the seal land.
  5. Record each reading and the affected item number.

Acceptance: No blade crack or casing contact; axial gap below 0.80 mm in service; runout below 0.05 mm TIR. Rebuild to installation values in Table 4-4.

7.6 Alignment verification TASK-INSP-006

Level: Field. Prerequisites: TASK-SAFE-001 complete; base fasteners seated; pipe strain checked. Tools: Laser alignment tool or two dial indicators, feeler gauge, torque wrench. Consumables: Motor shims, thread locker. PPE: Safety glasses, gloves, safety footwear.

  1. Clean the motor and pump coupling measurement surfaces.
  2. Measure cold parallel offset and angular difference at four shaft positions.
  3. Adjust motor shims and position until both values meet Table 4-4.
  4. Torque motor bolts to 120 N·m. Repeat the measurements.
  5. Record as-found and as-left values; reinstall the guard.

Acceptance: Offset ≤0.10 mm and angular difference ≤0.10 mm per 100 mm after final torque.

8.2 Replace the mechanical seal TASK-REM-002 / TASK-INST-002

Purpose
Renew a worn or leaking shaft-seal cartridge.
References
Sections 1.1, 4, 7.2, and 10; Figures 8-1 and 10-3.
Required parts
Item 08 for model A or item 09 for models B/C; items 11–13.
Required tools
Torque wrench, depth gauge, nonmetallic seal tool, dial indicator.
Consumables
Compatible cleaning solvent, lint-free wipes, new O-rings, assembly lubricant.
PPE
Safety glasses, chemical-resistant gloves, safety footwear.
Safety
Verify the seal chamber is vented and drained. Keep hands clear of sharp gland edges.

Removal

  1. Record the seal part number and leakage position.
  2. Remove the coupling guard and access cover. Mark the cartridge orientation.
  3. Loosen the retainer screws in an alternating sequence.
  4. Withdraw the cartridge along the shaft without scoring the sleeve.

Inspection

Measure face wear and shaft runout by Sections 7.2 and 7.5. Reject chipped faces and damaged O-ring grooves.

Installation

  1. Clean the chamber and fit new O-rings. Select the cartridge by Table 1-2.
  2. Slide the cartridge over the sleeve in its marked orientation.
  3. Seat the gland evenly. Torque item 13 screws alternately to 15 N·m.
  4. Rotate the shaft by hand and check for binding. Restore the cover and guard.

Alignment: Verify Section 7.6 after reassembly. Functional test: Run Section 6.2 and inspect the seal area for leakage. Return to service: Record cartridge and O-ring part numbers, torque, wear depth, and leak result.

Alignment: Complete Section 7.6 after coupling restoration. Functional test: Run Section 6.2 and compare P1/P2 readings with Table 4-2. Return to service: Record bearing batch, oil type, installed play, bolt torque, temperature, and vibration.

Items 23, 24, 36, 38–42 as condition requires.
Required tools
Impeller puller, dial indicator, feeler gauges, torque wrench.
Consumables
New casing gasket, lint-free wipes, assembly oil.
PPE
Safety glasses, cut-resistant gloves, safety footwear.
Safety
Drain and vent the casing. Support the rotor and protect blade edges.

Removal

  1. Mark rotation direction and impeller position.
  2. Remove item 42 cover bolts in a cross pattern and lift off item 23 cover.
  3. Release the retaining nut and washer. Remove the impeller with a puller.

Inspection

Complete Section 7.5. Record blade condition, shaft runout, and four axial-gap readings.

Installation

  1. Fit the correct impeller and key. Fit a new retaining washer.
  2. Torque item 39 retaining nut to 95 N·m.
  3. Measure the axial gap at four quadrants. Adjust to 0.30–0.60 mm.
  4. Fit a new cover gasket. Torque item 42 bolts to 85 N·m in a cross pattern.
  5. Rotate the shaft by hand. Check that no blade contacts the casing.

Alignment: Complete Section 7.6 if the drive train was disturbed. Functional test: Run Section 6.2; compare flow, current, noise, and vibration with baseline. Return to service: Record part number, gap readings, shaft runout, and test results.

REF-TERM-001

13. Glossary

Acceptance criterion
A stated value or condition used to accept an inspection result.
Alarm threshold
A measured level that starts investigation while the pump remains under controlled operation.
Alignment
The position of motor and pump shafts relative to each other.
Angular difference
The change in coupling-face separation across a stated span.
Axial gap
The clearance measured along the shaft axis between the impeller and casing.
Baseplate
The common support for motor and pump assemblies.
Bearing carrier
The housing that supports and lubricates the pump-shaft bearings.
Bearing radial play
Movement of a supported shaft across its axis under the specified check.
Blade
A shaped part of the impeller that transfers energy to air.
Casing
The fixed housing that contains the process-air channel.
Configuration
The model, serial range, and installed option set that determine service content.
Coupling
The assembly that transfers rotation from the motor shaft to the pump shaft.
Cover gasket
The replaceable seal between the casing and its cover.
Depot maintenance
Bench work that requires controlled disassembly and dimensional rebuild.
Discharge
The connection through which air leaves the pump.
Effectivity
The model and serial numbers to which an instruction or part applies.
Elastomer insert
The flexible element between the two coupling hubs.
Fault isolation
A sequence of checks used to narrow a symptom to a cause.
Field maintenance
Work on an installed unit after controlled isolation.
Free flow
Air capacity with no inlet pressure restriction.
Gland
The component that retains the seal cartridge at the casing boundary.
Hour meter
The counter used to record accumulated running time.
Impeller
The rotating part that moves air through the side channel.
Inlet vacuum
Inlet pressure below ambient pressure, shown as a negative gauge value.
Inspection point
A defined location for a repeatable measurement.
Installation limit
The permitted measurement range after assembly or adjustment.
Isolation
Controlled separation of the pump from energy and process sources.
Leakage
Unintended air or fluid transfer across a joint or seal boundary.
Lockout/tagout
The site method for controlling an isolation point and its status.
Maintenance interval
The maximum elapsed time or operating hours between scheduled tasks.
Model
A product variant identified by its letter suffix.
O-ring
A circular elastomer seal fitted in a groove.
Oil bath
The lubricant reservoir inside the bearing carrier.
Operating point
The combination of load, flow, vacuum, speed, and process condition during measurement.
Outboard bearing
The bearing farthest from the coupling side of the carrier.
Overhaul
Planned strip, inspection, renewal, and rebuild of an assembly.
Parallel offset
The lateral distance between two shaft centre lines.
Part number
The controlled identifier used to select a replacement item.
Process air
The dry air handled by the pump through inlet and discharge.
Publication revision
The controlled issue state of this manual.
Radial runout
The total indicator movement measured as a shaft rotates.
Rated load
The stated electrical or mechanical load at a specified condition.
Return to service
The documented release after inspection and functional test.
Root cause
The underlying condition that allowed a failure to occur.
Seal cartridge
A replaceable unit that seals the shaft at the casing boundary.
Serial number
The unique unit identifier used for effectivity.
Service bulletin
A controlled instruction that communicates a product service change.
Service limit
The measured value at which a component must be removed from service.
Shaft sleeve
The replaceable surface between the shaft and seal.
Side channel
The annular path in which the rotating impeller transfers energy to air.
Sight glass
The window used to check static oil level.
Temporary revision
A controlled change issued between full manual revisions.
Torque
A specified turning moment applied to a fastener.
Traceability
The recorded link between a unit, installed part, work order, and result.
Vibration velocity
The root-mean-square speed of vibration measured at a stated point.
Wear ring
A replaceable interface that helps maintain the impeller-to-casing gap.
Work order
The authorised record that defines and tracks maintenance work.
Work package
A complete task unit with preparation, steps, acceptance, and records.
Zero energy
The verified condition after isolation, venting, and shaft stop.
