1. Scope and use

This demonstration shows how a service manual can help a qualified technician find the right task, check prerequisites, identify hazards, complete work in sequence, and record the result. The fictional VSH-450 is described as a motor-driven vacuum pump with a casing, impeller, shaft, bearings, seal, coupling, and guard.

Intended audience

  • Service technicians performing inspection and component replacement.
  • Maintenance engineers planning service and reviewing equipment records.
  • Operators who need the approved start, stop, and abnormal-condition guidance.
  • Engineering and support staff who maintain product data and service information.

How to use this manual

Start with the safety information. Identify the equipment from its approved nameplate and configuration record. Follow only the task that matches the work order, and use the released drawings, limits, and procedures for the actual unit. If a required value or step is unavailable, stop and request approved information rather than estimating.

2. Safety information

Read the equipment-specific safety instructions and the site risk assessment before work. This sample cannot identify the hazards, energy sources, or controls for a real installation.

Hazards to assess before service

  • Electrical energy: identify every supply and control source. Use the site lockout/tagout process, then verify the equipment is in the required safe state.
  • Rotating parts: do not remove guards or approach a moving coupling. Confirm that rotation has stopped and cannot restart.
  • Vacuum and process energy: isolate and release stored pressure or vacuum using the approved system procedure. Do not loosen a connection to test whether it is pressurised.
  • Hot surfaces and process material: check the equipment state and process hazards. Use the approved cooling, draining, and personal-protection requirements.
  • Lifting and handling: use the approved mass, centre-of-gravity information, lifting points, and rigging plan. This sample supplies none of these values.
Stop-work rule: Stop if the equipment identity is uncertain, isolation cannot be verified, a required limit is missing, or the observed condition differs from the approved procedure. Escalate the issue to the responsible supervisor or engineer.

4. How the pump works

This conceptual diagram shows the fictional process path. Confirm the actual flow direction and process connections from the equipment drawing before work.

  1. Gas enters through the inlet connection.
  2. The motor turns the impeller through the coupling.
  3. The impeller and casing guide gas toward the discharge connection.
  4. The connected process system receives the discharge flow.

6. Installation

This section demonstrates procedure structure. It is not an installation instruction for real equipment. The installation drawing, foundation requirements, pipe loads, electrical design, lifting plan, and alignment limits are not part of this sample.

Before you begin

  • Confirm the equipment identity, configuration, work order, and approved installation procedure.
  • Review the site risk assessment, isolation boundary, lifting plan, and required personal protective equipment.
  • Check that approved foundation, alignment, piping, electrical, and process data are available.
  • Use only the tools, lifting equipment, and consumables listed by the released procedure.

Installation sequence

  1. Verify the installation location. Compare the foundation and surrounding clearances with the approved installation drawing. Record any discrepancy and stop until it is resolved.
  2. Position the assembly. Use the approved mass, lifting points, and rigging plan. Do not lift from the motor, coupling guard, or process connections unless the released handling drawing identifies them as lifting points.
  3. Secure the base. Follow the approved foundation and fastening procedure. Use only the specified fasteners and torque values.
  4. Connect process piping. Confirm the inlet and discharge against the system drawing. Support piping independently where required by the approved design.
  5. Complete electrical connections. Have qualified personnel follow the released wiring diagram and site electrical rules. Confirm protective devices and grounding against the approved design.
  6. Check alignment and guarding. Use the approved alignment method and tolerance. Install the coupling guard before the unit is released for operation.
  7. Record installation status. Complete the site checklist, attach inspection records, and resolve open items before commissioning.

8. Operating procedures

Use the approved operator procedure for the installed configuration. The following task structures illustrate how a manual separates routine actions from abnormal response.

Prepare the pump for an approved start

  1. Confirm that the equipment identity and operating configuration match the work instruction.
  2. Review the approved operating limits, process requirements, and site start-up checklist.
  3. Check that maintenance work is complete, tools are removed, access panels are restored, and guards are fitted.
  4. Confirm that alarms, protective devices, and process connections are in the condition specified by the approved procedure.
  5. Start the equipment only when authorised by the site procedure. Record the required operating observations.

Stop the pump

  1. Follow the approved normal-stop sequence for the connected process.
  2. Confirm the equipment reaches the required stopped state and record any abnormal noise, leakage, vibration, or alarm.
  3. For maintenance, apply the approved isolation and lockout/tagout process. A normal stop is not an energy-isolation method.

Respond to an emergency

Use the site emergency plan and the equipment-specific emergency-stop instructions. If a person is at risk, raise the site alarm and follow the emergency response process. Do not restart the unit until the cause has been assessed and an authorised person has released it.

12. Removal and component replacement

Each service task in a released manual should identify the configuration, hazards, prerequisites, parts, tools, ordered actions, verification, and record required. The examples below show that structure but intentionally omit torque, clearances, extraction methods, and other product-specific instructions.

Remove the pump assembly

Prerequisites: approved work order, equipment-specific isolation procedure, process drain and handling instructions, lifting plan, and released assembly drawing.

Tools and parts: use only the tools, lifting equipment, plugs, and replacement items listed in the approved task.

  1. Confirm the equipment identifier and record the installed configuration.
  2. Isolate electrical and process energy according to the approved site and equipment procedures. Verify the safe state.
  3. Drain, vent, cool, and disconnect the system only as specified by approved instructions.
  4. Label and protect disconnected lines and electrical conductors as required by the released method.
  5. Attach the approved lifting equipment at the specified lifting points. Remove the assembly using the approved handling plan.
  6. Protect open connections and record the unit condition, removed parts, and unresolved findings.

Reinstall the pump assembly

  1. Confirm that the replacement or repaired assembly matches the approved configuration.
  2. Inspect the foundation and mounting surfaces against the released installation drawing.
  3. Position and secure the assembly with the approved handling and fastening methods.
  4. Reconnect process and electrical interfaces using the released drawings and procedures.
  5. Verify alignment, guards, connections, and protective devices against their approved acceptance criteria.
  6. Complete commissioning checks and records before the authorised release to operation.

Replace a shaft seal

Prerequisites: isolation verified; process boundary made safe; correct seal kit and revision confirmed; released seal drawing and setting method available.

  1. Record the pump configuration and the seal-kit identifier before disassembly.
  2. Remove access components in the order shown by the approved assembly drawing.
  3. Inspect the shaft and seal chamber using the released acceptance criteria. Record damage or wear.
  4. Install the approved seal kit using its specified orientation, setting dimensions, lubricant, and assembly method.
  5. Reassemble the pump using the released fastener and alignment data.
  6. Perform the approved leak check and record the result before returning the unit to service.

Replace a bearing set

Prerequisites: isolation verified; bearing configuration and replacement set confirmed; approved extraction, fitting, lubrication, and clearance data available.

  1. Record the bearing identifiers and installed arrangement before disassembly.
  2. Remove the coupling, seal components, and bearing carrier in the order specified by the released assembly drawing.
  3. Remove the bearing with the approved tool and method. Do not apply force through rolling elements unless the approved procedure permits it.
  4. Inspect the shaft seat and carrier against the released acceptance criteria. Record damage or contamination.
  5. Fit the approved bearing set using the specified method, orientation, lubricant, and clearance data.
  6. Reassemble the shaft train and complete the specified rotation, alignment, and inspection checks. Record the results.

Replace the motor assembly

Prerequisites: qualified electrical personnel; isolation verified; motor nameplate, wiring diagram, approved replacement, and lifting method confirmed.

  1. Record motor identification, terminal connections, mounting arrangement, and coupling configuration.
  2. Isolate and verify every electrical source. Disconnect conductors and identify them as required by the released wiring procedure.
  3. Remove the motor using the approved lifting points and handling plan.
  4. Compare the replacement motor rating and interfaces with the approved configuration before installation.
  5. Install and reconnect the motor using released mounting, wiring, and alignment instructions.
  6. Have qualified personnel complete required electrical tests. Verify the guard and record the commissioning disposition.

Replace the coupling

Prerequisites: isolation verified; correct coupling set and revision confirmed; released removal, fit, and alignment instructions available.

  1. Record the coupling arrangement and available alignment measurements before removing components.
  2. Remove the guard only after the equipment is isolated and rotation has stopped.
  3. Remove coupling elements and hubs with the approved tools. Do not strike shafts or hubs unless the released method specifies that action.
  4. Inspect shaft ends and mounting interfaces against the approved criteria.
  5. Install the approved coupling set and align the shafts using the released method and tolerance.
  6. Reinstall the guard, complete the required checks, and record alignment results and part traceability.

Replace the impeller

Prerequisites: isolation verified; casing drained and made safe; impeller configuration, retention method, and approved replacement confirmed.

  1. Record the pump configuration, rotation direction, and installed impeller identifier.
  2. Remove the casing cover and retention hardware in the sequence shown by the released assembly drawing.
  3. Remove the impeller with the specified extraction method. Protect the shaft, seal surfaces, and casing.
  4. Inspect the shaft, key or retention interface, and casing using the released criteria.
  5. Install the approved impeller with the specified orientation, retention hardware, and assembly values.
  6. Verify clearances, rotation, balance status, and post-assembly condition against the approved data. Record the result.

These component tasks intentionally do not supply extraction forces, temperatures, torque values, clearances, or run-out limits. Add those only from verified product engineering data.

15. Glossary and records

Acceptance criterion
A documented requirement used to decide whether an inspection or test result is acceptable.
Configuration
The approved combination of equipment, components, and options that identifies a unit.
Isolation
A controlled process that separates equipment from energy or process sources before work.
Lockout/tagout
A site-controlled method for preventing unexpected energisation or release of stored energy during work.
SME
Subject-matter expert; a person who provides or verifies specialist technical information.
Work order
An authorised record that defines the work to be performed and its status.

Records to retain

  • Equipment identity and configuration.
  • Work order, isolation record, and personnel authorisation where required by site process.
  • Inspection method, instrument, measurement location, result, limit, and disposition.
  • Parts installed or removed, including approved identifier and traceability details.
  • Open technical queries, approved deviations, and release decision.
